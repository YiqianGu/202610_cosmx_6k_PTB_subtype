What to click. The Cell type layer opens on the 10 major types. Click the ▸ chevron on a row to expand that major into its subtypes and leave the others collapsed — so you can show TREM2⁺ macrophages at subtype level beside Fibroblast at major level, in one view. Expand all / Collapse all do every row at once. The dropdown also holds Activation (just the two populations of interest), transfer confidence, and sample. Each legend row has a colour picker — your choices persist in the browser — and a genes button listing that label’s top 20 marker genes; click a gene to colour cells by its expression. The ◎ button rings those cells, or paste cell IDs into the highlight box.

Read these caveats before quoting anything. The two slides are different specimens on different panel builds and different segmentations (median cell area differs by 31%), so a difference in composition between them is not interpretable as biology. CD3 immunofluorescence exists only on PTB 24.3 and CD68 only on PTB 22.2; CD45 is on both. Proliferating is a single-subtype label that the prior run showed absorbs T cells, so treat it with suspicion rather than as a proliferation measure. PTB antigen-activated T comes from only 112 reference cells (1.5% of the reference’s T cells), which is why the reported percentage is a bound rather than a point estimate.

The - V2 row is not a cell type. It is A02’s re-application of an archived rule intersecting the top 20% of cells on three gene modules and — on PTB 24.3 only — CD3 protein. Measured on this data its largest group is fibroblast (~28%), not T cell, and it agrees with the transferred label at about 1% precision (8–9× over chance). Its own author concluded it is “not a usable per-cell classifier”. PTB 22.2 has no CD3 channel, so its V2 call uses three criteria instead of four and falls below chance — the two slides’ V2 numbers are not comparable. The round-2 subtype call is untouched and remains the annotation of record; hover any cell to see both.
